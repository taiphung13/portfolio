Overview

Apollo is a marketing platform designed to unify how VNG Games’ studios manage campaigns from planning to execution. Before Apollo, teams were working across fragmented tools and siloed data, making it harder to align strategy, capture opportunities, and measure ROI with confidence.

Apollo brings these workflows into a single system, giving teams the structure, visibility, and insights they need to run more effective marketing campaigns.

Problem

Fragmented marketing operations slowed decision-making and execution

VNG Games’ studios were working with a marketing setup that was:

  • Hard to evaluate and forecast, making it difficult to assess game potential and plan with confidence.
  • Weak in planning, with limited support for building and comparing different business scenarios.
  • Limited in insights, reducing visibility into user behavior and campaign performance.
  • Fragmented across channels, making execution less efficient and ROI harder to measure.

Design Statement

How might we give game studio teams the tools and insights they need to make better marketing decisions throughout the campaign lifecycle?

Research

User interviews

We interviewed 12 stakeholders across studio teams, including heads of department and team members, to understand both data needs at the leadership level and usability pain points in daily workflows.

  • For head of department, they needed clearer visibility into forecasting, scenario planning, ROI, and campaign performance to make faster and more confident decisions.
  • For team member, they needed a workflow that was easier to use day to day, with better support for analytics, segmentation, testing, automation, and campaign optimization.

Key Problem

Problem Summary

The research revealed that the current campaign workflow was fragmented across multiple tools and did not support the different needs of operational and leadership users.

Head of department:Campaign data was spread across different platforms, making it difficult to get a clear view of performance, forecast outcomes, and make timely decisions.

Team member:Daily campaign tasks involved too many manual steps, tool switching, and dependency on other teams, which slowed down execution and increased the risk of errors.

Share problem:Both groups lacked a single, consistent platform that connected campaign planning, execution, and performance data in one clear workflow.

Main Insight

Findings from interview

Head of department:Needed clearer visibility into forecasting, campaign performance, and ROI to support faster decision-making.

Team member:Needed a simpler, more self-serve workflow for campaign setup, segmentation, testing, and optimisation.

Both:Needed a unified platform that reduced fragmentation and supported more confident, data-informed decisions.

Key Insights & Design Opportunities

Research highlighted three key opportunities:

Reduce manual work

Campaign creation involved repetitive tasks, multiple tools, and frequent support from the data team. Marketers needed a more self-serve workflow that would allow them to set up, review, and launch campaigns with fewer manual steps and less back-and-forth communication.

Create role-based experiences

Campaign creators and senior stakeholders had different goals when using the platform. Daily users needed a faster and more focused workflow for execution, while senior users needed visibility into performance, approvals, and overall campaign progress. A single interface could not support both groups effectively.

Validate campaigns before launch

Users could not easily check whether audience segments and notification content were set up correctly. Clear previews and validation were needed so they could identify mistakes before launching a campaign.

These opportunities shaped the major design decisions: separating creator and admin experiences, simplifying the segment creation flow, and improving the notification builder with clearer previews.

Before

Before the redesign, Apollo used a combined interface for both creators and admins. This meant users with very different goals had to navigate the same view, which made the experience more complex, less focused, and harder to use efficiently. We separated the experience into distinct creator and admin views so each user group could see the right level of information, actions, and workflow context for their role.

After

Major design decision #2

Made segment creation clearer and more self-serve

We redesigned the segment creation flow to give users a clearer step-by-step process and the ability to generate audience data directly through configurable conditions. The goal was to reduce reliance on the data team, lower the risk of setup errors, and make segment creation faster and more reliable for marketing teams.

Major design decision #3

70% of users swipe up after receiving the notification on mobile.

Research showed that the previous notification text was fixed and not customisable, limiting how well the message could be tailored to users. This indicates that the fixed, non-customisable notification copy may have reduced message relevance, leading many users to dismiss it rather than engage.

Title: Allows users to customise the notification headline, helping the message feel more relevant and attention-grabbing at first glance.

Message: Supports a more detailed notification body, including dynamic variables such as username, so the content can feel more personalised and contextual.

Image: Lets users add an optional image to enrich the notification visually and make the content more engaging.

Preview: Shows a real-time mobile preview across iOS and Android, helping users check how the notification will appear before launch.

Reflection

Learnings

This project strengthened my ability to design for complexity at the workflow level, not just the screen level. It taught me how to balance business rules, user needs, and system scalability, while using clearer structure and interaction design to reduce friction in high-frequency operational tasks.

Early developer involvement

In the past, I relied on my design lead or product manager to coordinate with developers. Because of that habit, I only consulted developers after designing the first feature for Apollo, which led to me redoing part of the user flows. Now, I involve developers early to confirm the design's feasibility and avoid rework.

Flexible mindset

Although I always strive for the best experience, I also learned that business goal often require compromises. In this project, I recognized the need for flexibility instead of getting stuck on perfecting every detail. I learn to identify design elements that can be simplified or postponed without sacrificing the core experience.

Reflection

Future Development

If the platform continued to evolve, I would explore four opportunities to make campaign planning more intelligent, connected, and scalable.

AI-assisted campaign setup

Use AI to suggest audience segments, campaign content, and channel recommendations based on previous campaign performance. Marketers would remain in control, but the platform could reduce repetitive setup work and help them get started faster.

Predictive performance insights

Introduce forecasting tools that estimate audience reach, engagement, and potential campaign outcomes before launch. This would help teams compare different scenarios and make more informed decisions earlier.

Cross-channel journey orchestration

Expand Apollo beyond individual campaigns by allowing marketers to plan connected customer journeys across push notifications, email, in-app messages, and other channels from one place.
